Oregon starting points · existing services

Find a living situation that fits your needs

Looking for a place to live, more support where you live now, or help leaving an unsafe situation? Different services handle different needs. Start with the situation closest to yours and contact the provider or referral agency to confirm eligibility and current options.

I need a group home or supported living

For people with intellectual or developmental disabilities, Oregon offers several possible residential arrangements, including 24-hour homes, adult foster homes, and supported living.

  1. Start with your county's developmental-disability program. In Washington County, call 503-846-4737 for intake or eligibility questions.
  2. Ask for a services coordinator and describe what support and living arrangements you would prefer.
  3. Explore the state's residential provider and vacancy information with your coordinator. Ask about licensing, accessibility, personal choice and actual availability.

See Washington County adult I/DD services. A listed vacancy is not a confirmed placement.

I want to stay in my own home with support

Supported living and in-home assistance may help with daily activities while preserving independence.

  1. Contact Washington County developmental-disability case management if I/DD services may apply.
  2. If you need aging, disability or long-term-care guidance, call the Oregon ADRC at 855-673-2372.
  3. Ask what assessments, support plans or funding programs are available and whether you may choose your service provider.
I need emergency shelter or transitional housing

In Washington County, shelter placements commonly begin with a referral rather than arriving unannounced at a residence.

  1. Contact Washington County shelter services or call Community Connect at 503-640-3263.
  2. Explain whether you are unhoused or at imminent risk of losing housing and ask how to enter the coordinated referral process.
  3. Ask about family needs, disability accommodations, waiting lists and alternative options if no placement is available.
I need to leave an abusive or dangerous living situation

Specialist advocates can help discuss confidential shelter and safety options. Publishing individual shelter addresses can create risks, so we link to established referral services instead.

  1. If danger is immediate, call 911.
  2. For confidential Oregon domestic-violence support, call Call to Safety at 888-235-5333.
  3. Ask the advocate for additional local support and shelter referral options. Their guidance can help avoid opening more sensitive pages on a shared device.

Privacy: Someone else may be monitoring your device or browsing activity. Consider a trusted device if you need to look for assistance discreetly.

Quick Exit: Use the fixed button at the lower left, or press Escape twice quickly, to replace this page with the Google homepage. This does not erase browser history, downloads, searches, phone records, or protect against device monitoring. The National Domestic Violence Hotline offers technology-safety guidance, but visiting an external site may create a revealing history entry. Consider using a device the other person cannot access.

Oregon · community support

Youth and family support

You deserve support and a chance to explore your identity and future without pressure. Whether you are looking for connection, housing help, someone to talk to, or guidance for a family member, these existing programs may be starting points. You do not need to tell Auralethi anything about yourself to use this guide.

Privacy comes first: Opening an outside organization’s website may leave a more revealing record in browser history or on a monitored device. If someone may be watching your activity, consider a trusted device and ask an advocate what communication options are appropriate. Calls and texts may also appear in device or account records.
I need someone to talk to right now

The Trevor Project: Crisis support for LGBTQ+ youth throughout the US, 24 hours a day. Call 866-488-7386 or text START to 678678. Trained counselors offer confidential support, with limits such as an imminent safety emergency or reporting obligations.

988: Call or text 988 for emotional or mental-health crises. If someone is in immediate physical danger, call 911.

Before opening an external link on a shared device, consider whether its website name might be noticed.

I want community or trusted people to connect with

SMYRC, New Avenues for Youth: A Portland-area resource center for LGBTQ+ youth ages 13–24, offering groups, community activities, basic supplies and support referrals.

  1. Call 503-872-9664 to ask about current hours, age requirements, virtual options and transportation accessibility.
  2. If you feel comfortable, ask about peer groups, counseling referrals or gender-affirming clothing.
  3. Official program site: SMYRC information (opens an external site).
I need housing or a place to stay

New Avenues for Youth: Provides youth housing and shelter-related programs in the Portland region, including an LGBTQ+ young-adult housing program. Not every program serves every age, and vacancies are not guaranteed.

Outside In: Has a young-adult day program with meals, showers and housing-navigation support for eligible youth ages 16–24. Call 503-432-3986 for current access instructions.

Ask a provider about eligibility, age restrictions, immediate shelter options and confidentiality before sharing personal details.

Program information: New Avenues housing (external) and Outside In (external).

I'm a parent, caregiver or trusted adult looking for guidance

You can help by listening without judgment, respecting privacy, asking what support is wanted, and connecting the young person with age-appropriate local services. If there is an immediate safety concern, seek prompt help from qualified professionals.

The Oregon Health Authority's youth and family resource overview (external) offers additional statewide starting points.

Auralethi does not operate these programs or provide crisis counseling, housing placement, or confidential messaging. Services and reporting obligations differ by provider. Please verify availability and privacy policies before sharing sensitive information.

Oregon · family and individual support

Autism and developmental support

Finding services should not require a family to know every agency name or navigate every system alone. Start with the kind of help you need. These pathways serve autistic children, teens, adults and people who care about them. You do not need to enter a diagnosis or personal details on Auralethi's site.

I'm worried about my young child's development (birth through age five)
  1. Review Oregon's Early Intervention and Early Childhood Special Education (EI/ECSE) starting page.
  2. Use the state's local referral-contact information to find the program serving your county, and ask how to request an evaluation.
  3. Ask what happens next, whether services are offered in a language or format your family needs, and how long an appointment may take.

Oregon EI/ECSE starting point (external). You do not need to guess a child's diagnosis before asking about a developmental concern.

My school-aged child needs educational support
  1. Contact your child's school or district and ask about requesting a special education evaluation, including how to put your request in writing.
  2. If there is an existing Individualized Education Program (IEP), ask for a meeting and copies of the current plan and evaluation records.
  3. If you need help understanding decisions or resolving a disagreement, contact the Oregon Department of Education special education support office at 503-947-5600.

Oregon special education questions and dispute support (external). School support is based on educational eligibility and individual needs, not simply a medical label.

I need help with daily life, home supports, or services after school
  1. Review Oregon's Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities (I/DD) service eligibility page.
  2. Contact your county Community Developmental Disabilities Program (CDDP) to ask how to apply and what records are needed.
  3. If found eligible, ask a service coordinator about a support plan, in-home help, choices of providers and transition planning.

Oregon I/DD eligibility and application (external) · In-home supports (external). Eligibility and available services are determined by the responsible program.

We need other families, peer support, activities, or a resource directory

The Autism Society of Oregon lists statewide and regional support groups for autistic people and families, as well as educational resources and a provider directory. Many listed groups do not require a formal diagnosis.

  1. Choose a regional or virtual support group that fits your needs.
  2. Ask about accessibility, age ranges, cost, registration and whether autistic people themselves help shape the group.
  3. Explore its resource directory for services relevant to your county.

Regional and virtual groups (external) · Resources for children (external) · Resources for adults (external).

Support should reflect each person's preferences, communication and sensory needs, and right to self-determination. Autism does not automatically mean crisis or that someone needs to be changed. External websites and calls can create records on shared or monitored devices. Auralethi provides referrals, not diagnoses, eligibility decisions or case management.

Your device · Your choices

Browsing privacy: what you can control

Important: If someone monitors your device, deleting history or changing settings may be visible to them and could increase risk. Consider using a trusted device at a library, workplace, or with someone you trust. You do not have to clean up your device to ask for help. The Quick Exit button remains available at the bottom of this page.

Chrome on Android
  1. Open Chrome, tap the three-dot menu, and choose Delete browsing data.
  2. Choose a time range. Check what will be deleted before continuing; the default may only cover recent activity.
  3. Use More options to review History, Cookies and site data, and Cached images and files. Select only what you intend to clear, then confirm.

Browser history is not the same as Google account search activity. If signed in and syncing, changes may affect other devices.

Safari on iPhone or iPad
  1. Open Settings → Apps → Safari.
  2. Choose Clear History and Website Data.
  3. Review the timeframe and confirm only if that is appropriate for your situation.

Clearing Safari does not erase records kept by websites, other apps, or someone monitoring the device.

Firefox on Android
  1. Open Firefox and tap the three-dot menu.
  2. Choose Settings → Delete browsing data.
  3. Review the categories you want removed, then choose Delete browsing data.

Firefox also lets you remove individual visited pages from its History list. Be aware that changes to history can be noticed.

What clearing browser history cannot do

It cannot reliably remove network logs, monitoring software, shared-account activity, downloaded files, screenshots, notification previews, call or text records, or another person's own records. Private browsing also does not make a monitored device safe.

If a device or account may be monitored, a specialist advocate can help you consider a safer way to access support. Avoid making sudden settings changes if they could put you at greater risk.

Auralethi is providing referral information, not housing placement, residential licensing, crisis intervention or care services. We have not inspected or endorsed the facilities referenced by these directories. Contact the responsible organizations for current requirements, safety information and availability.
